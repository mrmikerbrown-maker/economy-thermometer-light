Is the economy hot or cold?

Where each major economy has been on inflation and growth (or unemployment), and the path it took to get here. Pick a country and a business cycle.

Example data: made-up numbers, only for checking the layout.

How to read this
  • The chart plots inflation against either growth or unemployment, one dot per quarter (growth) or per month (unemployment). The trail runs from the start of the chosen business cycle to now, with arrows showing the direction of travel. The centre of the chart is the inflation target and the country's 10-year median for growth or unemployment.
  • Growth is real GDP growth year on year, not on the previous quarter, because quarterly figures jump around too much to draw a readable path. Inflation is headline consumer price inflation, the change over the last 12 months.
  • Ringed dots are recessions: the official NBER dates for the US and, elsewhere, two or more quarters in a row of falling GDP. Hollow dots are off the scale and pinned to the edge; hover or tap one for its real value.
  • The Textbook cycle button adds a dashed loop showing how a cycle runs in theory: recovery, boom, late cycle, downturn. It is a guide to the shape, not a forecast.
  • Targets used: 2% for the US, euro area, UK, Japan, Norway and Sweden, 1 to 3% for Canada and New Zealand, 2 to 3% for Australia and 0 to 2% for Switzerland (the chart is centred on the midpoint). The Fed targets a slightly different measure of inflation (PCE) that usually runs a little lower than the CPI shown here.
  • Timing: each figure is the latest published, so the dates differ between countries. Some are a few months old. Not trading advice.
  • Sources: Bank for International Settlements, OECD and national statistics offices via FRED, Eurostat and the US Bureau of Economic Analysis.
